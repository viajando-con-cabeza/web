Rome

What to see in Rome

The must-sees of the Eternal City, and a few off-the-beaten-path corners.

Roman Colosseum

The symbol of Rome. Buy your combined ticket for the Forum and Palatine Hill in advance to skip the line.

Roman Forum and Palatine Hill

The political heart of ancient Rome. Allow at least 2 hours to explore it at a leisurely pace.

Trevi Fountain

The square can be viewed for free, but starting February 2026, access to the area closest to the fountain will cost €2 for tourists and non-residents during regulated hours. Check the official conditions before you go.

Pantheon of Agrippa

General admission: €7 starting July 2026 (discounts and exemptions apply). There is no official tourist access without a queue. Check opening times and tickets on the Italian Ministry of Culture website.

Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel

Book a timed-entry ticket online; the queue without a reservation can exceed 2 hours.

St. Peter's Basilica

Free entry to the basilica; climbing the dome costs extra, but the views are well worth it.

Trastevere

The most atmospheric neighborhood in Rome, ideal for wandering aimlessly and dining at a local trattoria.

Piazza Navona

One of the city's most beautiful squares, featuring Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers at its center.

Castel Sant'Angelo

The ancient Mausoleum of Hadrian, offering spectacular views of the Tiber and St. Peter's Basilica.

Gianicolo

Rome's best free viewpoint, especially beautiful at sunset.
