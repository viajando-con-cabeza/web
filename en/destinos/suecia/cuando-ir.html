    OUR EXPERIENCE

    We visited Sweden in August, in the middle of summer, to explore its cities, landscapes, and the Stockholm archipelago. For now, it is the only month we have visited; select August to see our real photos and tips.

    ✈ WE TRAVEL IN AUGUST

    The advantage of summer was being able to combine the city and the archipelago

    On our trip, we didn't just stay downtown: we explored different neighborhoods, visited museums, and also headed out to the Stockholm archipelagoThe images on the side are from that part of the trip. For us, the beauty of traveling in August is being able to dedicate time to strolls and the water, without organizing the day solely around museums.

    What would we keep in mind if we were to return during these dates? Don't fix all your outdoor plans in advance: we would choose the most favorable day for the ferry and the viewpoints, and leave one or two indoor activities as alternatives. We don't have an exact record of how many days it rained during our stay, so we aren't going to make it up.

    THINGS TO DO BASED ON THE WEATHER
    ☀

    If the weather is good

    Prioritize the ferry, the Södermalm and Djurgården viewpoints. Make the most of the light for walks along the waterfront.

    ☂

    If a shower hits

    Have a fika or visit a nearby museum and save the viewpoints for when the weather clears. Changing your plans can save the day.

    ☁

    If it rains for several hours

    Spend the day at the Vasa Museum, Fotografiska, or exploring the subway art. Check transport and conditions before taking any ferry.

    MISTAKES TO AVOID

    Seven common mistakes when planning a trip to Sweden.

    Booking without checking events

    Concerts, fairs, and festivals can increase hotel demand. Check the event calendar and compare prices before setting your dates.

    Forgetting Midsommar

    At the end of June, there are celebrations and some museums or shops change their hours. If you want to enjoy the festivities, plan ahead; check opening times.

    Choosing summer without comparing dates

    July and August can have higher prices. Compare nearby weeks and weekdays: shifting your dates by a few days can help you save.

    Taking snow for granted

    January and February are cold, but snow is not guaranteed. Don't book a winter trip expecting to find the city completely covered in white.

    Ignoring daylight hours

    It gets dark very early in winter. Schedule your walks and visits to viewpoints during the midday hours; save some museums for the afternoon.

    Not checking the ferries

    Routes and frequencies to the archipelago change with the season. Check the outbound schedule and, above all, the time of the last return boat.

    Assuming Christmas lasts all December

    Christmas markets have specific dates, and some places reduce their hours around the holidays. Check what will be open during your visit.
