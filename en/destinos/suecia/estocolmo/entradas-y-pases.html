Our experience · VCC

IS A TOURIST PASS WORTH IT?

It depends on how many paid attractions you want to visit.

OUR EXPERIENCE · VCC WE DIDN'T BUY IT

In Stockholm, we only paid for the Vasa Museum and the Royal Palace. We prefer one or two paid visits per city and exploring the rest at our own pace; that's why the pass wasn't worth it for us.

Few visits: don't buy it

For one or two tickets, it's usually better to buy them separately.

Many visits: it might be worth it

If you concentrate several attractions into one or two days and the individual tickets cost more than the pass, it might be worth it. Check its validity.

Our rule: choose based on your tastes, your pace, and the actual cost.

How to save

HOW TO SAVE

Six decisions to enjoy Stockholm without buying unnecessary tickets.

Prioritize what truly interests you

Choose two or three paid tours you want to enjoy. Don't add museums just because they appear on a must-see list.

Buy on the official website

Check prices and opening hours before paying. At the Vasa Museum, you can buy upon arrival, but buying in advance does not allow you to skip the line.

Don't buy a pass out of habit

At VCC, we prefer individual tickets. Only compare a pass if you are going to do many paid visits in a short amount of time.

Alternate with free plans

Gamla Stan, the Södermalm viewpoints, and walks through Djurgården help you enjoy more without buying another ticket.

Group visits by area

Combine Vasa and Djurgården on the same day; save the Royal Palace for your tour of Gamla Stan. This will help you avoid unnecessary travel.

Check discounts and opening hours

Check rates by age and special dates. At Vasa, entry is free for those under 18; at Skansen, for those under 16. In all cases, please check the official terms and opening hours.
